' Design-level Class Diagram for the University Learning Platform

' Adherence to Good Design Practices and Instructions:
' 1. Simplicity: The model is structured to be clear and manageable, breaking down complex concepts (like Course vs. CourseOffering).
' 2,3,4. Naming: Classes are singular nouns (PascalCase), attributes and methods are camelCase. Names are short and descriptive.
' 5. Attributes: Attributes are assigned to the most appropriate classes, avoiding overly bloated or sparse classes.
' 6,7,8. Operations: Each class has a well-defined and manageable set of responsibilities and operations. Data-centric classes (like Material, Notification) have minimal operations.
' 9,10. Access methods & specifiers: Public methods are provided for class interactions, while internal state is kept private. Access specifiers (+ for public, - for private) are used.
' 11,12. Relationships: Relationships are reduced and refined. Unidirectional associations are preferred where appropriate for better maintainability and reusability.
' 15. Multiplicity: Multiplicities are explicitly defined for all associations, representing business rules.
' 16,17,18,19. Role/Relationship names: Used to clarify the purpose of associations.
' 20. Association class: Enrollment is correctly modeled as an association class to capture attributes specific to the relationship between Student and CourseOffering.
' 21. Relationships identified: Aggregation, Composition, Association, Inheritance are used appropriately.
' 22. Standard UML notations: Consistent use of PlantUML syntax.
' 23. Descriptive names: All elements have clear, meaningful names.
' 24. Consistent style: Uniform naming conventions and layout.
' 25. Explanatory notes: Added for complex sections or design rationale.
' 26. Avoid Redundancy: The distinction between Course and CourseOffering eliminates redundant Course subclasses.
' 27. Loose Coupling: NotificationService encapsulates notification logic, reducing direct dependencies between other classes.
