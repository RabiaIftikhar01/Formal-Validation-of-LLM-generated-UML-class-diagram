My design philosophy for this task focuses on:
1.  **Clarity and Simplicity:** Avoiding over-engineering while ensuring all core requirements are met.
2.  **Separation of Concerns:** Distributing responsibilities logically among classes (e.g., `Course` vs. `CourseOffering`, `Notification` vs. `NotificationService`).
3.  **Loose Coupling:** Minimizing dependencies between classes to improve maintainability and reusability.
4.  **Extensibility:** Designing classes and relationships that can adapt to future changes without major refactoring.
5.  **Adherence to UML Best Practices:** Consistent naming, multiplicity, and relationship types.

Here's the rationale for key design decisions:

*   **User Hierarchy:** The `User` abstract class serves as a base for `Student` and `Instructor`, encapsulating common attributes and behaviors like `login()` and `logout()`. Each role (`Student`, `Instructor`) then extends `User` and defines its specific actions and responsibilities.
*   **Course vs. CourseOffering:** To address the requirement that "course offerings can vary by level" and "instructors create and release course offerings," I've introduced two distinct classes:
    *   `Course`: Represents the abstract definition of a course (e.g., "Introduction to Computer Science"). It has a `CourseLevel` enum to specify its academic level.
    *   `CourseOffering`: Represents a specific instance of a `Course` being taught during a particular `semester` and `year`. It includes details like `maxStudents`, `courseContent`, and `OfferingStatus`. This separation allows for multiple offerings of the same course and makes the system more flexible.
*   **Enrollment as an Association Class:** The `Enrollment` class effectively models the relationship between a `Student` and a `CourseOffering`, capturing specific details like `enrollmentDate` and `EnrollmentStatus`. This adheres to the practice of using association classes when the link itself has attributes.
*   **Notification System:**
    *   `Notification`: Encapsulates the details of a single notification message (ID, message content, timestamp, type).
    *   `NotificationService`: A dedicated service class responsible for the logic of creating and sending notifications. This decouples the act of sending from the entities that trigger notifications (e.g., `Instructor`).
*   **Enums for Status and Type:** `CourseLevel`, `OfferingStatus`, `EnrollmentStatus`, and `NotificationType` are modeled as enums to provide clear, constrained, and self-documenting values, improving data integrity and readability.
*   **Unidirectional Associations:** Relationships are refined to be unidirectional where appropriate (e.g., `CourseOffering` *references* a `Course`, but `Course` doesn't necessarily need to know all its offerings directly at this level of detail for simplicity).
*   **Attribute and Operation Visibility:** Attributes are typically private (`-`) to enforce encapsulation. Core operations are public (`+`) for interaction. Accessor (get/set) methods are assumed to exist for private attributes as per good practice #9, but are not explicitly listed for brevity.
*   **Instructor Responsibilities:** The `Instructor` class contains methods that reflect their core authorized actions, such as `createCourseOffering()`, `updateCourseMaterial()`, and `sendCourseNotification()`. These methods would internally interact with `CourseOffering` and `NotificationService` to fulfill their tasks.
*   **Student Responsibilities:** The `Student` class includes methods for their primary interactions, like `enrollInCourse()`, `viewCourseContent()`, and `trackMyEnrollments()`.

This design aims to be easily understandable by developers, testers, and future maintainers, providing a clear blueprint for implementation.
